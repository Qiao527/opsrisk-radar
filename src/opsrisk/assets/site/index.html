      How scores are produced

      Feeds from trade publications are fetched daily. Each article is normalized, deduplicated (same URL, or a near-identical headline within 7 days) and scored from its title and summary only.

      The default scorer (rules-v2) asks three questions, following a written labeling guide:

      1. Event: does the article report something interrupting goods, capacity or production (closure, strike, attack, accident, outage, severe weather, congestion, a dated trade measure)?
      2. Status: is it ongoing, or resolved, speculative or postponed?
      3. Article type: is it earnings, a deal, an appointment, a launch or an opinion piece?
      SeverityScoreMeaning
      High / critical7 – 10An interruption is under way or has a stated date
      Medium4 – 6.9Risk-relevant but not interrupting: resolved, threatened, proposed, or a trend
      Low0 – 3.9Supply chain context or not a risk signal

      Every event shows the phrases that triggered its score and the reason. An optional hybrid mode sends non-noise articles to an LLM that must quote the article verbatim; it is off unless the method column says otherwise.

      Limitations

      • Scores are automated and unreviewed. They describe headlines, not verified facts, and are not advice.
      • Evaluation so far uses machine-suggested labels; human labeling is pending. See the evaluation set.
      • Coverage is limited to the configured English-language trade feeds.
